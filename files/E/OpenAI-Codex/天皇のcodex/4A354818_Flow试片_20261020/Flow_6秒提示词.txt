Create a six-second, single-take, photorealistic commercial shot for a small sticky-note product.

Reference roles:
Use the first image for the office environment, computer design, lighting, materials and photographic style.
Use the second image ONLY for the cyan-blue paper appearance and the rectangular proportions of one individual sticky note. Do not bring the stack of pads or its white background into the scene.

Art direction:
A refined, believable daylight workspace. Preserve the elegant aluminum monitor, restrained screen reflections, tactile oak desk and layered background depth from the scene reference. The cyan-blue sticky note is the main color accent. It is one thin matte paper sheet at the monitor's lower-right bezel, approximately 48 mm wide and 35 mm tall, horizontal rather than square. Its face is blank. Keep the note physically attached and stable throughout.

Camera choreography:
0–1.5 seconds: Begin slightly wider than the scene reference, showing the monitor, keyboard and desk. A softly defocused notebook edge occupies a small portion of the lower-left foreground.
1.5–4.8 seconds: Make one smooth diagonal dolly move forward and slightly to the right, with a restrained pan that keeps the blue note visible. Create real parallax between the foreground notebook, monitor and distant window. The note gradually becomes the visual focus; this must feel like a physical camera move through space, not a flat digital zoom.
4.8–6 seconds: Ease gently into a close three-quarter view of the note and monitor corner. Keep enough of the bezel and stand visible to explain the use case. Hold the final composition for about half a second.

Maintain natural perspective, realistic hardware geometry, delicate contact shadows, subtle material detail and consistent exposure. Use moderate depth of field: make the paper clear without turning the environment into featureless blur.

No people, hands, extra sticky notes, product stacks, logos, captions or generated writing. No cuts, dramatic orbit, whip pan, floating paper, morphing computer parts or sudden lighting changes. No dialogue or music. The purpose is to demonstrate convincing real-world materials, thoughtfully directed camera movement and a premium stationery-ad atmosphere.
